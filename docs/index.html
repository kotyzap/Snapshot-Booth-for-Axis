How it works

The visitor's side

Five steps, one phone, zero downloads

This is everything a visitor sees, from the poster on the wall to the photo in their inbox.

Scan the printed QR

One poster next to the camera. The code is static and belongs to that camera, so you print it once and forget about it. A preview screen with a QR on the video is optional.

  • Any phone camera opens booth.4xs.dev/i/…
  • Rotating one-time QR available for the screen
  • Or a battery e-paper QR box next to the camera

Type an e-mail, pick a background

The page opens in the visitor's language. They enter an e-mail, choose one of your backgrounds and agree to a plain consent line that says when the photo will be deleted.

  • Backgrounds are your CamOverlay graphics, shown by name
  • Consent text and page copy editable per language

Get ready with a live preview

When it's their turn, the phone shows what the camera sees, about two frames a second, so people can step into the frame before the countdown starts. Anyone who scans during a shot waits in line and sees their place.

  • Preview only for the visitor whose shot is active
  • One shot at a time per camera, the rest queue

Count down on three things at once

The number runs on the phone, on the preview screen and on the camera itself: the front status LED blinks red faster and faster, then stays on while the photo is taken. Nobody has to look at their phone to know when to smile.

 slow → medium → fast → steady

The photo lands in their inbox

The visitor gets an e-mail with the full-resolution photo and your frame on it, plus a link to download or delete it. Photos are removed automatically after the retention period you set.

  • Sent from booth@mail.4xs.dev in the visitor's language
  • "Delete it now" link in every e-mail

Under the hood

The camera does the work. The cloud just passes notes.

The camera sits behind your router and asks the booth server for work. No port forwarding, no video upload, no PC next to the camera.

Frame burned into the photoThe photo is taken from a running MJPEG stream at the booth's resolution (up to 4K), where CamOverlay graphics are drawn. A one-shot snapshot would leave the frame out.
Works behind NATThe camera long-polls the server, about 3,500 small requests a day per camera. The preview screen streams over the local network only.
Privacy by defaultPhotos and addresses are deleted after 3 days (you choose). Visitors can delete their photo at any time. The QR is hidden while a photo is taken.

Setup

Five steps to the first photo

  1. Install the app on the cameraAs an ACAP (.eap) or as a CamScripter package.
  2. Get a PIN at booth.4xs.devSign up with your e-mail, add the camera and enter the PIN under General info on the camera.
  3. Design the frame in CamOverlayAt the booth's photo resolution. Each background can switch one or more overlays.
  4. Print the QR posterOptionally open the preview screen on a TV or tablet on the camera's network.
  5. Take a test photoThe settings page checks the LED and capture without sending anything.

Requirements: ACAP on AXIS OS 12.10+ (ARTPEC-7, ARTPEC-8, ARTPEC-9, CV25) or the CamScripter package (ARTPEC-6, ARTPEC-7, ARTPEC-8, ARTPEC-9, CV25), internet access from the camera. CamOverlay for the frame. Bench-tested on AXIS Q1656 and M1075.

FAQ

Do visitors need to install anything?

No. The QR opens a web page. Any recent phone browser works.

Will my frame and logo be in the photo?

Yes. The photo comes from the camera's own stream at the booth's resolution (up to 4K), which includes your CamOverlay graphics. The QR code and countdown digits are kept out of it.

What stops someone from taking hundreds of photos?

Each e-mail address gets 3 photos per camera per rolling 30 days by default; you can change the number and add unlimited addresses for staff. On top of that each camera takes at most 60 photos per hour, and a reusable QR box code opens at most 5 sessions.

What happens when two people scan at once?

The second visitor joins a queue and sees their position. Each gets their own photo.

Is there an alternative to a printed poster?

Yes. Show the QR on the preview screen's video, or put a battery e-paper QR box next to the camera (CamScripter package). The box shows a code that changes on a timer, sleeps outside opening hours and is set up over USB-C from the camera's settings page.

Where are photos stored, and for how long?

On Cloudflare storage, for 3 days by default. You choose the retention per camera. Every e-mail has a link to delete the photo right away.

Does the camera need a public IP or open ports?

No. The camera connects out to the booth server, so it works behind any normal router or firewall that allows HTTPS.

Which cameras are supported?

ACAP: AXIS cameras with ARTPEC-7, ARTPEC-8, ARTPEC-9 or CV25 on AXIS OS 12.10 or newer. CamScripter package: ARTPEC-6, ARTPEC-7, ARTPEC-8, ARTPEC-9 and CV25. The chip is listed in the camera's System → About. Photo resolution: up to 4K on ARTPEC-8, ARTPEC-9 and CV25, up to 1440p on ARTPEC-6 and ARTPEC-7; never above what the camera itself offers.
